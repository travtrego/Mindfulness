# The Re-entry template

## Why the existing five didn't fit

**Rehearsal was the near miss.** It has an approach, an event, and an aftermath — and this
session does contain a feared moment that gets met. But Rehearsal puts the event at the
peak and winds down after it.

That structure would break this session. Ending shortly after the conversation says
*the conversation was the event*. What the listener wants is *that happened, and then I
went back to my workpapers.*

**So the signature of Re-entry is: the feared moment is not the climax, and the session
continues past it into more ordinariness than preceded it.** Beat 8 is longer than beat 7
on purpose.

**The other four were not close.** Immersive goes somewhere; this stays. Reflective has no
narrative; this has a day. Anchored place returns to a refuge; a workplace is not one.
Breath-only has no content.

## Beats

| # | Beat | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Grounding intro | cached; sensitive variant when flagged |
| 2 | Settling | must state that nothing is a test |
| 3 | Arrival | the ordinary approach — mundane detail, deliberately |
| 4 | Threshold | the crossing-in. Usually the anchor's source |
| 5 | Competence | the thing the listener can still do. **Never absent** |
| 6 | The moment | the feared exchange, embedded not climactic |
| 7 | What comes back | the response. Small, not a speech |
| 8 | **Continuation** | **the load-bearing beat.** Longer than 6 and 7 |
| 9 | Anchor | drawn from a daily-recurring physical event |
| 10 | Reorientation | |

## Target state: unremarkable

Every other template aims at something. Rehearsal aims at competence, Immersive at wonder,
Reflective at presence. **Re-entry aims at nothing happening.**

That inverts the craft problem. Elsewhere the risk is generic prose failing to be vivid;
here the *content itself* is mundane, and the vividness has to come from precision about
ordinary things — the cold concrete holds in summer, the pale-worn elevator button, the
chair settling an inch.

The ordinariness is not the backdrop. It is the therapeutic payload.

## Categories it serves

Going back to work · returning after illness · seeing people after a loss · back to a gym
after injury · anywhere the listener was away from and must now simply *be* again.

# Craft notes

**The badge beep is the strongest thing in the session** and it earned its place by being
literally true: it happens every morning, takes half a second, is identical for everyone,
and *knows nothing about you*. An anchor should be a real recurring physical event, not a
symbol invented for the session.

**The answer is never scripted.** Beat 6 says *"you tell them something true — not the
whole of it, just the true part you actually wanted to say."* Writing the words would be
furnishing the room (§4). The listener supplies them, and they will be better.

**"Notice that your voice stayed yours the whole way through"** does the work the session
is actually for. The fear is not the question; it is being turned into something else by
answering it.

**Beat 7 ends on their weekend.** That line is the point of the whole beat — for the other
person the conversation is already over, and it was never the size it was for the listener.
Shame insists it is the largest thing in the room. It isn't.

**No reassurance anywhere.** The session never says *they don't think that about you.*
Arguing with the belief validates it as a proposition worth arguing. Instead it puts the
listener somewhere the belief has nothing to attach to — a chair, a screen, numbers going
where they should.

**Competence before exposure.** Beat 5 exists so the listener is standing on something
before beat 6 arrives. This is the same structural principle that lets session 01 promise
the lift: **earn it first** (`SPEC.md` §8.2). Never run the feared moment before the
listener has been reminded of what they can still do.

# Schema changes needed

1. **`template` enum gains `reentry`.** Six templates.
2. **New beat roles:** `threshold` · `competence` · `the_moment` · `what_returns` ·
   `continuation`.
3. **`continuation` must be at least as long as `the_moment` in words.** A validator rule,
   not a guideline — an outline that peaks and stops has failed at exactly the thing this
   template exists to do.

   **This rule caught its own reference session.** The first draft of beat 8 ran 87 words
   against 90 for beat 6: the session whose entire thesis is *the day keeps going* had made
   the continuation its shortest movement. Extended to 127 words with more ordinary
   afternoon — a microwave, a pointless meeting, leaving at the normal time. The structure
   now says what the session says.
4. **`sensitivity_flag: true` should force the sensitive intro variant.** Currently the
   spec says it *may*; it should be automatic.
