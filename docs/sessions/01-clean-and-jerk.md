# Craft notes

## Rules exercised

**Name the sense, not the furniture.** *"The bar is colder than the room"* gives a
temperature and lets the listener supply the bar. *"The blue Eleiko bar with red plates"*
would furnish it for them. Never a plate colour, a bar brand, a weight in kilos, or a
description of the crowd — all of it is theirs.

**Guided incompleteness in a specific scenario.** The test was whether it survives contact
with a real named venue. It does, because the specificity lives in *sensation*, not
*scenery*: convention center air being cooler than it should be, sound arriving before
sight. The room is never described.

**Contrast and asymmetry.** *"Cold, then not"* on the chalk. *"Slower than you wanted"*
against *"it doesn't stop."*

**Noticing language.** *You notice your hands before you notice anything else about
yourself.* Active perception. The two places it drops to plain declarative —
*"And then you stand"*, *"It doesn't stop"* — are deliberate: the lift is the one moment
that shouldn't be observed from outside.

**Vividness never demanded.** *"You don't have to build it. It'll be there."*

**Silence carries 42% of the runtime.** The longest holds are placed after the stand
(11s) and after the anchor lands (12s), not distributed evenly.

## Where the exclusion did work

*No missed lifts, no bombing out.* Two places the draft wanted to go there and didn't:

- Beat 3 could have named the stakes of a third attempt. Instead: *"there's a number next
  to it and you don't need to read it."*
- Beat 5's difficulty is real but never framed as risk of failure — *"a place where it
  could have stopped"* is past tense by the time it's said. The outcome is never in doubt
  in the narration, only in the body.

## Where the obstacle beat went

The template says *"one realistic thing goes wrong; an adaptive response follows."* With
failure excluded, that relocated **inside** the successful lift: the clean is slow out of
the bottom, the adaptive response is waiting for the bounce instead of rushing, and the
stand is a grind. Nothing goes wrong; something is hard and is met.

This is more accurate to the sport than a miss would have been. A max third attempt that
moves smoothly isn't a PR.

# What this exposed about the schema

Real findings from writing it. All five should change the outline JSON before anything is
generated.

**1. Silence needs a type, not just a duration.** A 5s sensory pause and an 11s
post-climax hold are different instruments and should not both be `{"silence": n}`. The
schema needs `sensory` · `transition` · `open` · `hold`.

**2. Beats need a `do_not_mention` field.** Exclusions are enforced per beat, not per
session. Beat 3 is where "third attempt" pressure would naturally surface; the constraint
has to be present *in that beat's context*, not just in a session-level header the model
may have drifted from by beat 6.

**3. The Rehearsal template's beat name is wrong.** `obstacle_and_recovery` presumes
something fails. Rename to **`difficulty_and_response`** — which covers both a genuine
setback and difficulty-within-success, and doesn't force a miss into a session where the
user asked for none.

**4. Word count per beat varies more than expected** — 78 to 168, and it's not
proportional to duration. The approach beat is long and airy; reorientation is short and
dense. The schema should carry `word_target` and `silence_total` as **independent**
fields; deriving one from the other produces the wrong pacing.

**5. Delivery rate belongs to the beat, not the session.** Computing this at one uniform
95 wpm gets 14:26, but beats 4–6 should be delivered at scene-building pace (65–90 wpm) —
the setup and the lift are exactly where hurrying would wreck it. That pushes the real
runtime toward the 15-minute end. A single session-level `wpm` field produces the wrong
pacing at precisely the moments that matter most.

**6. The soundscape must be able to drop to nothing.** Beats 5 and 6 want near-silence —
room tone at most. A stem system that always has something playing would ruin the lift.
The schema needs an explicit `ambience: none` state, not just a low volume.

# Open question for the next exemplar

Beat 7 says *"on Saturday you'll do it and some part of you will remember doing it here."*
That's a claim about transfer, and it's the strongest sentence in the session.

It's also the closest this script comes to a promise. Worth deciding as policy whether
that register is allowed, because generated sessions will reach for it constantly — it's
the most satisfying place a rehearsal can end, and it's one step from an outcome claim.

Current instinct: allowed when it points at a **behaviour** (you'll take the breath),
prohibited when it points at a **result** (you'll make the lift). This one stays inside
the line. Barely.
